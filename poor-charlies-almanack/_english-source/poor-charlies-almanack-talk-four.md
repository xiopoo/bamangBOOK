# Talk Four: Practical Thought about Practical Thought?

> 来源：https://www.stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-four

---

Editor's warning, as suggested by Charlie: Most people don't understand this talk. Charlie says it was an extreme communication failure when made, and people have since found it difficult to understand.

---

What I'm going to try to do today is to extend the remarks I made two years ago at the USC Business School. The title of my talk is "Practical Thought about Practical Thought?" with a question mark at the end. In a long career, I have assimilated various ultrasimple general notions that I find helpful in solving problems.

The first helpful notion is that it is usually best to simplify problems by deciding big no-brainer questions first. The second helpful notion mimics Galileo's conclusion that scientific reality is often revealed only by math as if math was the language of God. The third helpful notion is that it is not enough to think problems through forward. You must also think in reverse, much like the rustic who wanted to know where he was going to die so that he'd never go there. The fourth helpful notion is that the best and most practical wisdom is elementary academic wisdom. But there is one extremely important qualification: You must think in a multidisciplinary manner.

If you rely entirely on others, often through purchase of professional advice, you will suffer much calamity. It is not usually the conscious malfeasance of your narrow professional adviser that does you in, but your failure to understand his limitations. The fifth helpful notion is that really big effects, lollapalooza effects, will often come only from large combinations of factors.

## The Practical Problem

It is 1884 in Atlanta. You are brought before a rich and eccentric Atlanta citizen named Glotz. Glotz offers to invest $2 million in 1884 dollars for a Glotz Charitable Foundation, in a new corporation organized to go into the non-alcoholic beverage business and remain in only that business forever. The other half of the new corporation's equity will go to the man who most plausibly demonstrates that his business plan will cause Glotz's foundation to be worth a trillion dollars 150 years later. You have 15 minutes to make your pitch.

## Charlie's Solution / Pitch to Glotz

The big no-brainer decisions: First, we are never going to create something worth $2 trillion by selling some generic beverage. We must make our trademarked name a strong, legally protected trademark. Second, we can only get to $2 trillion by starting in Atlanta, succeeding in the rest of the United States, and then rapidly going worldwide.

We will next use numerical fluency. By 2034 there will be about 8 billion beverage consumers in the world. Each of these consumers will be much more prosperous in real terms than the average consumer of 1884. Given that a single serving of a new-beverage might be priced to create 4¢ of profit, 8 billion consumers each buying 3.125 servings per year would give us $1 trillion in annual profit. This seems possible if we can create a beverage with strong universal appeal.

We must cause a new-beverage market to have strong, universal appeal, and we must also create a formidable dominance in our new market. Our simplifying no-brainer decision is that we must rely on a strong trademark. This leads to an understanding of the essence of our business: to create and maintain conditioned reflexes.

How does one create and maintain conditioned reflexes? The psychology text gives two answers: 1) by operant conditioning and 2) by classical conditioning, often called Pavlovian conditioning.

For operant conditioning rewards: We need to include rewards in all practical categories - food value in calories, flavor, texture, aroma, the stimulant effects of caffeine and sugar, and cooling effects for hot weather. To start, it is easy to decide to design our beverage for consumption cold. There is much less opportunity to counteract excessive heat compared with excessive cold. It is also easy to decide to include both sugar and caffeine.

For Pavlovian conditioning, powerful effects come from mere association. The neural system of Pavlov's dog causes it to salivate at the bell it can't eat. We will want every person and his dog to associate drinking our beverage with everything they like. Such extensive Pavlovian conditioning will cost a lot of money, particularly for advertising. We will spend big money as far ahead as we can imagine.

We will also use social proof - that powerful "monkey see, monkey do" aspect of human nature. By combining Pavlovian conditioning, social proof effects, and a wonderful-tasting, energy-giving, stimulating, and desirably cold beverage that causes operant conditioning, we will get a lollapalooza result.

Logistics and distribution strategy: There are only two practical ways to sell our beverage: as syrup to fountains and restaurants, and as a complete carbonated-water product in containers. We will need many bottling plants scattered over the world. We will maximize profits if we own the bottling plants and charge for syrup. Being unable to get a patent or copyright on our super-important flavor, we will work obsessively to keep our formula secret.

Final reality check - thinking in reverse like Jacobi: 1) We must avoid the protective aftertaste that causes consumers to stop drinking. 2) We must avoid ever losing even half of our powerful trademarked name. 3) We must avoid bad effects from envy by making our beverage cheap and widely available. 4) After our trademarked flavor dominates, we must avoid making any huge and sudden change in our flavor.

That is my solution to turning $2 million into $2 trillion. How consistent is it with the real Coca-Cola Company? The real Coca-Cola Company followed so much of the plan that it is now worth about $125 billion and will have to increase its value at only 8 percent per year until 2034 to reach $2 trillion.

## Educational Implications

Large educational implications exist if my answer is roughly right. The brilliant and effective executives who have run Coca-Cola with glorious success have also displayed glaring ignorance of psychology. Such extreme ignorance in both academia and business is a lollapalooza effect demonstrating grave defects in academia.

Academic psychology, while admirable and useful as a list of ingenious experiments, lacks intradisciplinary synthesis. There is a truly horrible lack of synthesis blending psychology and other academic subjects. The New Coke fiasco occurred where Coca-Cola's executives came to the brink of destroying the most valuable trademark in the world.

Consider the University of Chicago economics department, which has enjoyed a near monopoly of Nobel Prizes in economics. It is imitating Darwin, who spent much of his long life thinking in reverse as he tried to disprove his own hardest-won ideas. The Darwinian approach, with its habitual objectivity, is a mighty approach. Einstein said that one of the four causes of his achievement was self-criticism.

## Talk Four — Revisited

In this talk I attempted to demonstrate large, correctable, and important cognitive failures in US academia and business. My 1996 talk failed to get through to almost all people hearing it. The best explanation is that I displayed gross folly as an amateur teacher. I attempted too much. Readers can correct somewhat for my teaching defects by rereading Talk Four after mastering the final talk.
